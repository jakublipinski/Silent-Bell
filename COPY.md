# App Store copy

Every string that appears on the App Store product page, in all five languages the app ships. This is the source of truth: App Store Connect is where it is entered, but this is where it is written and reviewed.

Character limits are hard caps enforced by App Store Connect. Counts below were measured, not estimated. **Paragraphs are single lines on purpose**: App Store Connect preserves line breaks, so hard-wrapped text would show ragged breaks on the store page. Paste each block as-is.

| Field | Limit | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Name | 30 | Highest ASO weight |
| Subtitle | 30 | Second highest |
| Keywords | 100 | Comma-separated, no spaces between items |
| Promotional text | 170 | Editable without review |
| Description | 4000 | Barely indexed; does the selling |

**No emoji in Description or What's New** — App Store Connect rejects them. Name things in words instead (the silent taps are named, not marked).

**Never repeat a word between name, subtitle and keywords.** Apple indexes each term once, so repetition spends the budget twice for one result.

## Category

**Primary: Health & Fitness. Secondary: Productivity.** Health & Fitness is where the mindfulness audience browses, including Apple's own Mindfulness app. Productivity is the honest second home for the attention framing.

## A note on the translations

German, Japanese, French and Spanish were machine-translated by Claude, then reviewed a second time by Claude for idiom, platform vocabulary (Apple's own names for Focus modes, About screens and so on) and typography. They have **still not been reviewed by a native speaker**. The app is sold largely on the quality of its writing, so treat these as a strong first draft that a human translator improves, not as finished copy. The in-app strings carry the same caveat.

Conventions followed: German and Spanish use the informal *du* / *tú*, French the formal *vous*, all matching Apple's own localisations. French uses the typographic apostrophe and non-breaking spaces before two-part punctuation and inside guillemets. Japanese puts a space between Japanese and Latin words but none before counters like 分.

The word *Silent Bell* is a brand and stays untranslated everywhere, including inside sentences.

## Why the description says what it says

The **AN HONEST NOTE** section is load-bearing and must survive every edit. The hourly pause is the single most predictable one-star review this app can attract. Stated up front it reads as honesty; discovered cold it reads as a defect. Do not let it be trimmed for being off-message.

The closing line about public source code does double duty: it substantiates the privacy claim, which is otherwise unverifiable in a compiled binary, and it justifies the price to a reader who wonders why a small app costs anything.
